How Indian B2B Buyers Actually Search Online: Lessons from Naukri and IndiaMART Data

By Vikas Goyal  ·  August 2026  ·  5 min read

If you have only ever studied B2B search behaviour through Western SaaS marketing playbooks, Indian buyer and recruiter search patterns will surprise you in at least three specific ways. I have watched query logs across two very different platforms — a recruitment marketplace and a buyer-seller marketplace — and the patterns that repeat across both are more instructive than anything I have read in a generic marketing report.

Vernacular and Mixed-Language Search Is Bigger Than Most Marketers Assume

A meaningful share of search queries on both Naukri and IndiaMART blend English category terms with Hindi or regional-language modifiers, or use transliterated regional words entirely. A recruiter searching for "ghar baithe job Delhi" or a buyer searching "sasta wholesale rate near me" is not an edge case in Tier 2 and Tier 3 India — it is a substantial and growing share of total query volume, and it is almost entirely ignored by B2B marketers who build content and landing pages exclusively in formal English. Any Indian B2B marketing strategy targeting SMB or non-metro audiences needs to account for mixed-language and vernacular search intent explicitly, not as an afterthought localisation project but as a core part of keyword strategy from day one.

Mobile-First Search Behaviour Compresses the Decision Window

The overwhelming majority of search sessions on both platforms happen on mobile, often on mid-range Android devices with variable connectivity. This changes buyer behaviour in a very specific way: mobile searchers scan far less content before acting than desktop searchers did in the platforms' earlier years. A buyer on a 4G connection in a Tier 3 town is not going to scroll through a long, richly formatted page before making contact. They are scanning the first two or three results, checking for a recognisable trust signal, and acting. This compresses the effective "content real estate" a marketer has to earn a click or a conversion down to almost nothing, and it is why concise, scannable, mobile-optimised content consistently outperforms long-form desktop-style content for this audience segment.

Voice and Typed Query Structure Differ Meaningfully

As voice search grew on both platforms, we saw query structure shift from clipped keyword fragments ("packing machine Delhi") toward fuller natural-language questions ("which company sells packing machine near Delhi"). Marketers optimising purely for old-style fragment keywords increasingly miss this longer, more conversational query pattern, particularly among first-time digital users in smaller towns who are often more comfortable speaking a query than typing one.

Recruiter and Buyer Search Both Skew Heavily Toward Local Intent

Even on platforms built for national reach, both recruiter and buyer search carry a strong implicit local bias. A recruiter hiring for a Pune warehouse role searches with Pune-specific modifiers even when not required to. A buyer sourcing raw material searches with a nearby city or state modifier far more often than a purely national query. This local bias is stronger outside the top four or five metros, where logistics cost and relationship comfort weigh more heavily on the decision. Marketers building national campaigns without local search variants are systematically underserving a very large share of actual query volume.

A number that reframed how I think about this: in query analysis across both platforms, queries carrying an explicit or implicit geographic modifier made up well over half of total non-branded search volume outside the top metro cities, compared to a much smaller share within them. Geography is not a filter Indian B2B buyers apply after searching — for most of the country, it is baked into the query itself.

Trust-Qualifying Terms Show Up Inside the Query, Not Just in Post-Search Filtering

A pattern that surprised even experienced marketers on our teams: buyers and recruiters increasingly embed trust-qualifying terms directly into their search query — "verified supplier," "genuine company," "no fees job" — rather than applying trust filters only after seeing results. This tells you something important: trust anxiety is not a downstream conversion-stage concern to be solved with a badge at the last mile. It is present at the very first moment of intent formation, which means trust signalling needs to be reflected in how you title and describe your offering, not just in how you decorate the final decision page.

What This Means for Building Indian B2B Search Strategy

  • Build keyword strategy around mixed-language and vernacular variants, especially for SMB and non-metro targeting, not just formal English terms.
  • Design for mobile scanning, not desktop reading. Assume you have seconds, not minutes, to earn engagement.
  • Bake geography into your primary keyword targets, rather than treating location as a secondary filter layered on afterward.
  • Reflect trust signals in titles and early copy, since trust-qualifying language now shows up inside the search query itself.
  • Watch for the shift toward natural-language, voice-influenced queries and build content that answers full questions, not just keyword fragments.

The biggest mistake I see Indian B2B marketers make is importing a search strategy built for a different market's search behaviour and assuming it translates. It does not. Indian B2B and recruitment search behaviour has its own logic — shaped by language diversity, mobile-first access, and a real and justified trust deficit — and the marketers who study that logic directly, rather than assuming it mirrors global patterns, consistently win more of the available search volume.
