SEO Consultant for B2B SaaS Companies in India: What the Engagement Should Look Like

By Vikas Goyal  ·  August 2026  ·  7 min read

B2B SaaS SEO in India has a specific shape that generic SEO advice usually misses: the buyer is often technical enough to bypass sales for as long as possible, the sales cycle involves a free trial or demo request as the real conversion event, and competitors are frequently comparing themselves directly against each other in public content. An SEO consultant working with an Indian SaaS company needs a playbook built around these specifics, not a generic content calendar.

The Content Hierarchy That Actually Drives Trials

Comparison Pages ("X vs Y")

These are the highest-converting page type for most B2B SaaS companies because the searcher has already decided to evaluate the category and is choosing between named options. Indian SaaS companies frequently under-invest here out of a fear of naming competitors directly, but leaving the comparison unaddressed just means the competitor's own comparison page (which is rarely fair to you) becomes the only version a buyer reads.

Alternative Pages ("X Alternatives")

Buyers actively looking to switch tools search this pattern constantly, and it converts at a high rate because it signals active dissatisfaction with an existing vendor, not passive research.

Use-Case and Integration Pages

"[Product] for [industry]" and "[Product] + [tool] integration" pages capture searchers who have already decided on the category and are checking fit, which is a much shorter distance to a trial signup than a broad educational article.

Technical SEO Specific to SaaS Products

SaaS marketing sites frequently share infrastructure with the product app, which creates crawl and indexing issues generic SEO audits miss: staging subdomains accidentally indexed, documentation pages competing with marketing pages for the same keywords, and JavaScript-heavy pricing pages that don't render cleanly for crawlers. An SEO consultant for an Indian SaaS company should audit the product-adjacent technical surface, not just the blog.

Where GEO and AEO Fit for a SaaS Company

SaaS buyers are exactly the audience most likely to ask an AI tool "is [Product] good for a 50-person Indian logistics company" before ever visiting the website. Comparison and alternative pages that are already structured as direct answers ("Product A costs X, Product B costs Y, the main difference is Z") tend to perform well on AI citation for the same reason they convert well on traditional search: the content is inherently structured as a direct comparison rather than a narrative.

A metric SaaS companies underuse: trial signups attributed to organic search by page type, not just total organic traffic. A comparison page generating 200 visits a month with a 4% trial conversion rate is worth more than a blog post generating 5,000 visits a month with a 0.1% conversion rate, and budgeting decisions should follow that math, not the traffic number.
