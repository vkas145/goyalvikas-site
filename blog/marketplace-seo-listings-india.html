Marketplace SEO: How Buyer Search Behaviour Works on Platforms Like IndiaMART

By Vikas Goyal  ·  August 2026  ·  5 min read

Sellers ask me constantly why their listing does not show up when they search their own product name. The honest answer is usually that they are searching the way a seller thinks about their product, not the way a buyer searches for it, and on a marketplace platform that gap is the entire ballgame. I spent years watching search query logs at IndiaMART, and the single biggest lesson is that buyer search language and seller listing language diverge more than most sellers ever realise.

Buyers Search by Problem and Specification, Sellers List by Brand and Category

A seller lists their product as "XYZ Industries Heavy Duty Conveyor Belt." A buyer searches "PVC conveyor belt 500mm width food grade." Notice how little overlap there is. The buyer is searching with the specification that matters for their purchase decision — material, dimension, application — while the seller has listed with brand-first language that means nothing to someone who has never heard of the company. On IndiaMART, listings that led with specification-rich, buyer-language titles consistently outperformed brand-first listings in search visibility and click-through, often by a wide margin, regardless of how established the brand actually was offline.

This is the core discipline of marketplace SEO: write the listing title and description in the vocabulary of the search query, not the vocabulary of the product catalogue.

Long-Tail Specification Queries Convert Better Than Category Queries

A buyer searching a broad category term like "packaging machines" is usually still in early research. A buyer searching "automatic pouch packing machine 10-50 gm" has already narrowed their requirement and is close to a purchase decision. Sellers chasing visibility on broad category terms are competing against hundreds of listings for buyers who are not ready to transact. Sellers who structure their listings to rank on precise, long-tail specification queries are competing for far less traffic, but that traffic converts at a dramatically higher rate because the buyer's intent is already specific.

In our category analysis, listings ranking primarily on long-tail, specification-heavy queries saw enquiry conversion rates 2 to 3 times higher than listings ranking mainly on broad category terms, even though the broad-term listings received more raw impressions.

Structured Attributes Matter More Than Prose Description

On a marketplace, the search and filter system relies heavily on structured attribute fields — material, size, capacity, certification, minimum order quantity — not just free-text description. Sellers who fill every relevant structured field thoroughly become eligible for far more filtered search combinations than sellers who leave fields blank and rely on a well-written paragraph. A beautifully written description with empty attribute fields is functionally invisible to a buyer using filters, which is how most serious B2B buyers narrow a marketplace search after the first query.

A pattern I saw repeatedly: listings with 80 percent or more of relevant structured attribute fields completed appeared in roughly 3x more filtered search result sets than listings with under 40 percent completion, even within the identical product category. Attribute completeness was a stronger predictor of search visibility than description length or even listing age.

Freshness and Responsiveness Feed the Ranking Signal

Marketplace search algorithms, like any modern search system, weight recency and engagement signals alongside relevance. Listings that are updated periodically, and sellers who respond quickly to buyer enquiries, tend to rank more favourably over time than static listings from sellers who log in rarely. This creates a practical incentive structure: treat your marketplace listing as a living asset that needs periodic tending — refreshed photos, updated specifications, prompt responses — rather than a one-time setup task.

Geographic Precision Changes What Buyers See

A large share of B2B marketplace search on IndiaMART carries an implicit or explicit local intent — buyers frequently prefer suppliers within their state or a reasonable shipping radius, both for logistics cost and for the comfort of a nearer relationship in case of disputes. Sellers who accurately tag their service area, rather than leaving it generic or nationwide by default, show up more precisely in geographically filtered searches, and precise geographic matches tend to convert better because the logistics friction the buyer is worried about is already resolved.

A Practical Checklist for Marketplace Listing SEO

  • Lead titles with specification, not brand. Material, size, application — in the buyer's language, not the internal product code.
  • Complete every structured attribute field. Blank fields remove you from filtered searches entirely.
  • Target long-tail specification queries deliberately. Lower volume, dramatically higher conversion.
  • Update listings on a regular cadence. Freshness is a ranking signal, not just good housekeeping.
  • Tag geography precisely. Accurate service-area data matches you to genuinely local, higher-conversion buyer searches.
  • Respond fast. Response rate and speed feed directly into how favourably the platform's algorithm treats your listing over time.

Marketplace SEO is a different discipline from open-web SEO, because the "search engine" is a closed system optimising for successful transactions, not just relevance. Sellers who understand that the platform's algorithm is trying to predict which listing will actually satisfy the buyer — not just which listing best matches the keyword — end up building listings that win on both fronts simultaneously.
